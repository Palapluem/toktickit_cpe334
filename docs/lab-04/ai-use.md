## My Reflection

I used the specification and coding agents for different parts of the work. The specification phase helped turn inherited requirements into explicit decisions before implementation; the coding phase produced tests, changes and evidence against those decisions. I still owned the choices and approvals, and the reviewer—not the author—performed the merges.

The strongest safeguard was independent verification. A confident initial diagnosis was wrong; another audit claim was only partly supported; and mutation checks uncovered tests that looked complete but still let faults survive. Reproducing findings against the named source taught me to accept evidence, not confidence. I also had to distinguish staging-candidate results from final-main results and keep those report slots pending until the release gate.

For the next lab, I would verify test isolation before every run, preserve a concise decision log and check each test against the contract. AI assistance can speed up analysis and implementation, but responsibility for decisions, review and honest reporting remains mine.
